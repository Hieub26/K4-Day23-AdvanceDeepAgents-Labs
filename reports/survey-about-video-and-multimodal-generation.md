# Video and Multimodal Generation: A Survey of Models, Conditioning, and Evaluation

## TL;DR
- Video generation has moved from adversarial and discrete-latent sequence models toward diffusion and Transformer-based systems; current formulations increasingly treat video as a sequence of compressed spatiotemporal tokens. [1][2][3][4]
- Diffusion and autoregressive generation are not cleanly separate camps: latent diffusion Transformers, causal video diffusion, and cube-based autoregressive models explore different trade-offs in parallelism, temporal structure, and speed. [5][6][7][8]
- The field is broadening from text-to-video to systems that condition on images, video, masks, depth, pose, or actions, and to models that generate synchronized audio and video. [9][10][11][12][13]
- Persistent difficulties include long-horizon consistency, faithful action and camera control, efficient long-sequence attention, and evaluations that localize errors without losing broad coverage. [14][15][16][17]

## Background
Video generation models learn distributions over video sequences, often conditioned on inputs such as text, images, or actions; unconditional models instead approximate the video data distribution. [18] Early GAN work separated temporal structure from frame appearance: TGAN first generated a latent trajectory and then decoded its elements into frames. [1] Discrete-latent Transformers later compressed video with a VQ-VAE and modeled the resulting codes autoregressively, reducing the sequence modeling burden relative to pixels. [2] Diffusion methods extended image-generation machinery to video, while subsequent systems increasingly use spatiotemporal latent representations and Transformer architectures. [3][4] These advances matter because the goal is no longer simply to produce plausible short clips: recent work also targets multimodal alignment, controllable editing, extended temporal coherence, and interactive world simulation. [18][17]

## Representations and generation architectures
A useful distinction is how a model represents video and how it generates the representation. TGAN decomposes video into a temporal sequence of per-frame latents and an image generator; VideoGPT instead encodes spatiotemporal blocks into discrete VQ-VAE tokens and predicts them with a GPT-like Transformer. [1][2] The former emphasizes a smooth latent trajectory that supports interpolation, while the latter formulates generation as next-token sequence modeling. [1][2]

Diffusion approaches extend denoising over time as well as space. Video Diffusion Models use a 3D U-Net with factorized spatial and temporal attention, and report that joint image/video training improves sample quality and optimization relative to video-only training in their experiments. [3] Make-A-Video transfers text-image alignment from pretrained text-to-image models and learns motion from unpaired video, avoiding a requirement for paired text-video data. [19] Later Transformer variants move denoising into latent space: Latte uses a latent diffusion Transformer, while VDT applies spatial-temporal Transformer blocks and mask modeling. [20][5]

Autoregressive generation also continues to evolve rather than disappearing with diffusion. ViD-GPT adds causal generation and a KV cache to video diffusion, while NOVA reports an autoregressive design without vector quantization and VideoAR predicts spatiotemporal cubes rather than producing one frame at a time. [6][7][8] The summaries report favorable long-video, efficiency, or temporal-coherence results for these approaches, but do not provide a common set of quantitative comparisons; the available evidence therefore supports architectural diversity, not a definitive ranking. [6][7][8]

## Conditioning, control, and multimodal output
Text conditioning was first enabled in part by transferring text-image knowledge to video generation. [19] Newer systems broaden the conditioning interface. Wan2.1 describes text-to-video, image-to-video, video editing, and video-to-audio tasks; its VACE workflow can use optional video, mask, and image inputs, with depth or pose conditions available for editing workflows. [12] Camera control is another explicit goal: VD3D uses spatiotemporal embeddings to control camera movement in video diffusion Transformers. [21] For action-conditioned prediction, DreamTrue renders action trajectories into image-space conditions and applies geometric calibration; its authors identify limited examples of unsuccessful interactions as a source of biased predictions and introduce counterfactual post-training to broaden coverage. [9]

Audio-video generation is developing along both conditioned and joint-generation paths. MMAudio synthesizes audio from video and optional text, jointly trains on video, audio, and text, and adds a synchronization module to align visual conditions and audio latents. Its paper reports improvements from joint training across Fréchet Distance, Inception Score, ImageBind, and synchronization measures. [10] 3MDiT instead proposes tri-modal diffusion over text, audio, and video, with feature-level fusion and an option to adapt a pretrained text-to-video backbone. [11] DreamX-Creator describes native joint audio-video generation in which audio and video streams are separately processed before cross-modal attention couples them. [13] These designs differ in whether audio is synthesized from an existing video or co-generated with video, and in when and how the modalities exchange information. [10][11][13]

## Evaluation, efficiency, and interactive world models
Reported performance depends on the task and evaluation protocol. Foundation-model and paper summaries often claim state-of-the-art or improved results, but do not always expose task-level numbers or comparable evaluation setups; for example, summaries for VD3D and Video World Models with Long-term Spatial Memory state their aims or performance characterizations without quantitative results. [21][14] MMAudio provides several relative metric changes for its joint-training and synchronization components, illustrating more component-specific evidence, though those gains are tied to its evaluation setting. [10]

Evaluation design itself is an active concern in adjacent multimodal tasks. OmniCapBench identifies a trade-off between broad whole-caption evaluation and localized probes, while warning that unconstrained language-model judges can be unstable. [15] RISEBench and RISEBench++ organize visual-editing assessment around a fine-grained hierarchy that includes temporal reasoning. [22] For generation efficiency, MC-Sparse studies how sparse attention reduces latency in long-sequence diffusion Transformers, and attributes quality degradation at high sparsity to token grouping, inaccurate interaction selection, and dropped attention contributions. [16] These results underscore a central tension: lower computational cost and broad aggregate scores do not by themselves establish temporal, localized, or instruction-level fidelity. [15][22][16]

The scope of video generation is also expanding toward persistent interactive simulations. Matrix-Game 3.5 presents geometry-aware, real-time interactive world generation with patch memory as a step toward long-horizon simulation. [17] Video World Models with Long-term Spatial Memory similarly targets consistency through geometry-grounded memory. [14] Together, these directions shift evaluation from judging isolated clip appearance toward testing whether generated worlds remain coherent under extended interaction. [14][17]

## Trends and open problems
The recent trajectory is toward unified systems: variable-form visual inputs, editing and control, synchronized audio-video, and interactive world models are increasingly addressed within connected model families rather than as text-to-video alone. [11][12][17][13] At the same time, claims of progress need to be interpreted in light of differing tasks and incomplete quantitative reporting; benchmark proposals explicitly highlight weaknesses in global caption scores, localization, and unconstrained judging. [21][14][15][22]

Long-term temporal and geometric consistency remains unresolved, particularly when a model must preserve a scene while responding to new actions or camera movement. [14][9][17] Action fidelity is also linked to data coverage and calibration: DreamTrue identifies both poorly calibrated action conditions and scarcity of failed interactions as problems. [9] Efficient long-video generation presents a quality-speed trade-off, as sparse-attention work documents failure modes when interactions are pruned too aggressively. [16] Finally, multimodal systems must align events across streams, not merely generate each modality plausibly; synchronization modules and tri-modal fusion architectures are direct responses, but the cited work does not establish a single settled design. [10][11][13] Progress will therefore depend on evaluations that jointly test temporal coherence, condition following, cross-modal alignment, efficiency, and performance under extended interaction. [15][22][16][17]
